# Thrust-model aiding works, but published systems kept depth

The literature agrees on the basic recipe. A vehicle model turns actuator inputs into a velocity relative to the water. That velocity is fused into an error-state inertial filter as an aiding measurement, and sea current is estimated as filter states. Across many groups this has cut free-inertial drift by one to two orders of magnitude: in one abstract snippet, about 700 m of error fell to about 6 m on a HUGIN segment with no position aiding, and in another abstract over 1 km per hour fell to under 100 m of uncertainty per hour on low-cost SandShark AUVs. **However, every published model-aided system found by this review kept a depth/pressure sensor**, and most also had a DVL, USBL, LBL or ADCP for at least part of the mission. The user's setup has none of these: a VN-200 only, a MEMS IMU, GNSS only at the surface, no depth sensor, and eight BlueROV2-Heavy T200 thrusters. That is harder than any configuration reported. The vertical channel is unconstrained underwater. Current and model error cannot be separated within a single dive. No observability, consistency or drift result exists for this case. The filter machinery itself is well documented. Solà's quaternion ESKF (read in full) and Fossen's MSS toolbox use the same Hamilton, scalar-first, body-to-NED convention. The VN-200, however, outputs a **scalar-last** quaternion. Its internal INS reportedly stops updating position and velocity after about 45 s without GNSS. So the user's own external filter has to do all of the submerged navigation. The open gaps listed at the end are also candidate research contributions, but several design decisions have to be made first.

### Architecture: a separate model feeding a velocity measurement, with current as states

The abstracts agree that "the output from an experimentally validated kinetic vehicle model is integrated in the navigation system to provide velocity aiding for the INS", "together with real-time sea current estimation" ([JOE 2011 abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224238828_Model-Aided_INS_With_Sea_Current_Estimation_for_Robust_Underwater_Navigation)) [abstract/snippet]. A secondary snippet says that the current "is included as a state in the Kalman filter" [abstract/snippet, low confidence on wording]. The more detailed architecture is **[UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION]**: an error-state feedback filter in the HUGIN/NavLab style; the kinetic model run outside the KF; a measurement formed by rotating the model's water-relative velocity with INS attitude, adding the estimated current, and differencing against INS velocity. This is "model as pseudo-sensor", as opposed to "model inside the process model". The exact error-state vector, measurement equations, noise values and current process model (Gauss–Markov, NED, horizontal only, also [UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION]) **could not be verified**.

The HUGIN model was calibrated "from semi-empirical relationships, open-water test, and from navigation data" ([MIC 2007 PDF](https://www.navlab.net/Publications/Towards_Model_Aided_Navigation_of_Underwater_Vehicles.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. The UT'07 work fitted it by least squares to field data [abstract/snippet]. No evidence was found of online hydrodynamic-parameter states in Hegrenæs's filter. That suggests offline identification plus noise tuning [inference]. The model's DOF, coefficient list and propeller law (Fossen-form M ν̇_r + C ν_r + D ν_r + g = τ with rpm and fin inputs) are [UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION].

### What the results show, and what they depended on

The one hard number recovered is from MIC 2007. On a segment without position aiding, "the traditional INS breaks down quickly with a maximum Euclidian norm of the position error close to 700 meters, while the model-aided INS continues to perform excellently with a maximum norm of the position error of only 6 meters" ([MIC 2007 PDF](https://www.navlab.net/Publications/Towards_Model_Aided_Navigation_of_Underwater_Vehicles.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. Segment length, and whether DVL or pressure were still present, could not be verified. **There is a conflict between the notes on the JOE 2011 figures.** The Hegrenæs note says JOE numbers were inaccessible. The field-map note quotes a snippet giving about 6000 m over 3000 s with about 0.02% DRMS final error, describing it as a full-aiding/DVL-dropout scenario, not model-only ([navlab PDF](https://www.navlab.net/Publications/Model_Aided_INS_With_Sea_Current_Estimation_for_Robust_Underwater_Navigation.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. Treat that figure as unconfirmed context, not as a model-only benchmark. A separate snippet of "4% vs 1.5% of distance travelled" probably belongs to a later low-cost-AUV paper and **should not be cited as Hegrenæs**.

The decisive fact for the user is in the JOE abstract. The model aid was combined with "additional aiding sources including ultrashort base line (USBL) acoustic positioning, pressure readings, and measurements from a Doppler velocity log (DVL) with bottom track". The test scenarios removed USBL and/or DVL, but **the pressure sensor stayed in** ([JOE 2011 abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224238828_Model-Aided_INS_With_Sea_Current_Estimation_for_Robust_Underwater_Navigation)) [abstract/snippet]. The thesis presents the model as a backup against "velocity sensor failures or dropouts" ([NTNU Open](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/260288?show=full&locale-attribute=en)), not as a sole sensor. The setup also differed in kind: HUGIN is a large finned torpedo cruising at about 1.6–2 m/s, with what was [UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION] a navigation-grade ring-laser-gyro IMU. A slow, finless, 8-thruster vehicle has to rely on the T200 thrust curve and low-speed damping, which are usually less certain [inference].

## The field has moved toward fixing model error, and depth is always present

### Engelsman & Klein name model-based aiding as its own class

"Information Aided Navigation: A Review" by Daniel Engelsman and Itzik Klein (IEEE Trans. Instrum. Meas. 72, 2023; [arXiv 2301.01114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01114)) [abstract/snippet] sorts aiding into three groups. **Direct** aiding turns platform knowledge into pseudo-measurements, such as motion constraints. **Indirect** aiding extracts extra information from external sensors. In **model-based** aiding, "a vehicle simulation runs in parallel to the INS model", and the two feed each other so that their errors can be estimated. This appears to be the first survey to make model-based aiding its own category [inference]; the older surveys, Kinsey et al. 2006 and Paull et al. 2014 ([PDF](https://uml.iut.nsysu.edu.tw/hhchen/Handouts/2014%20AUV_Navigation_and_Localization_A_Review.pdf)), fold it into dead reckoning. Its underwater thread, from snippets of [the PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.01114) [abstract/snippet], runs as follows. Hegrenæs (2008) formulated both the hydrodynamic model and real-time sea current. Lammas (2010) used a vehicle-dynamics model during acoustic outages. Allotta (2016) used a Typhoon-AUV hydrodynamic model inside a UKF. Arnold (2018) used a FlatFish hydrodynamic model to bridge DVL dropouts. The section headings, the full reference list and any open-problems section **could not be read**. One search summariser misattributed Hegrenæs's MA-INS to Engelsman & Klein; the researchers corrected that.

### Since 2018: either fix the model's error or replace the model

After Arnold & Medagoda's ICRA 2018 paper ([arXiv 1805.08011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08011)) [abstract/snippet], the work splits two ways. That paper ran a manifold UKF with drag and thrust parameters carried as filter states "to account for the correlated nature of vehicle model parameter error", plus ADCP aiding and a tactical-grade IMU. The first branch keeps the physics model and attacks its parameter error. Randeni et al. identify a Gavia model by recursive least squares from propeller thrust and gyro rates, and re-tune it with a calibration mission ([Nonlinear Dynamics 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-017-3941-z)) [abstract/snippet]. Ji et al. 2023 **re-correct thrust-model coefficients online at each surfacing** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4251644)) [abstract/snippet]. A 2026 JMSE paper corrects the propeller-to-velocity map online with LBL and uses **pressure-derived vertical velocity** to correct attitude-induced model error ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14/7/612)) [abstract/snippet; authors not captured]. Martinez et al. 2015 estimate sea current with a 3-DOF linear model on the HRC-AUV ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5772/60415)) [abstract/snippet]. The second branch replaces the model with learned regressors: Saksvik et al.'s RNN from IMU, pressure and control inputs ([arXiv 2110.00661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00661)); the Klein group's BeamsNet family; and PiDR ([arXiv 2601.03040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03040)) [abstract/snippet]. Most of these still need a DVL for training or for partial input. No review dedicated to DVL-free model-aided navigation was found. The most recent survey, Damari … Klein (May 2026), focuses on AI-aided INS/DVL fusion ([arXiv 2605.04672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04672)) [abstract/snippet].

### Closest prior art: a six-thruster vehicle that recalibrates at the surface

**Ji, Cheng, Zhou & Li (Ocean Engineering 276, 2023)** is the nearest match to the user's architecture. Their small surface/underwater vehicle has six thrusters. At the surface it fuses GPS and the dynamic model in an adaptive KF; underwater it runs a dynamic-model-aided INS; and "thrust model coefficients were corrected online by the S-ASUV surfacing" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4251644); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801823004754)) [abstract/snippet]. **Whether it had a pressure sensor is unverified.** No drift numbers were recovered. On BlueROV2-class vehicles, every navigation paper found uses a Water Linked DVL-A50 or is not model-aided. Examples are the Cascade IPG Observer ([arXiv 2504.15235](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15235)) [abstract/snippet] and Naik et al.'s situation-triggered EKF on a BlueROV2 ([arXiv 2607.10597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10597); sensor suite not captured) [abstract/snippet]. Balasubramanian et al. found that a dynamic-model EKF beats a kinematic one on a simulated thruster-driven RexROV, but say it "needs improvements before it can be used in real time" ([arXiv 2105.12309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12309)) [abstract/snippet]. For BlueROV2-Heavy model parameters, the verified sources are Chu-Jou Wu's 2018 Flinders thesis (note the author name; it is not "Chao-Jen") ([PDF](https://flex.flinders.edu.au/file/27aa0064-9de2-441c-8a17-655405d5fc2e/1/ThesisWu2018.pdf)) and von Benzon et al.'s open-source simulator, whose "hydrodynamic parameters and thruster model have been validated in a test facility" ([JMSE 2022, Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-Open-Source-Benchmark-Simulator:-Control-of-a-Benzon-S%C3%B8rensen/6148ab3a4db300c0cfbb543ae62210e25dbef960); [GitHub](https://github.com/ROV-Simulator/ROV-Simulator)) [abstract/snippet]. Their parameter values could not be extracted. **No paper was found that uses a T200 thrust model as a navigation aid**; T200 models appear only in control and simulation work.

## The ESKF machinery is settled once the conventions are reconciled

### Solà gives a complete local-error ESKF (full text read)

Solà (arXiv 1711.02508; PDF dated 12 Oct 2017) [full text] uses the **Hamilton** quaternion: scalar first, right-handed, passive, q = q_GL, so x_G = q⊗x_L⊗q*. Errors are **local** (right-multiplied), q_t = q⊗δq. He warns that Hamilton and JPL quaternions can have equal values but "mean and represent different things", "the source of great confusion" ([PDF](https://github.com/TurtleZhong/msckf_mono/blob/master/Quaternion%20kinematics%20for%20the%20error-state%20Kalman%20filter.pdf), §3.1). The nominal state is 19-D [p v q a_b ω_b g] and the error state is 18-D. Error dynamics are eq. (237); the Euler transition matrix F_x is (269); the impulse covariances Q_i are (261)–(264). Solà says the mean prediction (267) "always returns zero" and can be skipped, but "you should NOT skip the covariance prediction (268)!!". He notes that gravity can be fixed instead of estimated (the "classical approach"). Correction uses H = H_x·X_δx, with the 4×3 block Q_δθ (280). Injection is q ← q⊗q{δθ̂}. The reset is P ← GPGᵀ with G = blkdiag(I₆, I − [½δθ̂]×, I₉) (287). Solà notes that most implementations use G = I, and says the full G "might be of interest for reducing long-term error drift". Footnote 26 says the (I − KH)P update has "poor numerical stability" and recommends the Joseph form. §7 gives the global-error variant; Table 4 lists exactly which blocks change. He cites Li & Mourikis (2012) for its "better properties", but gives no quantitative comparison. Two caveats: pdftotext dropped minus signs, which the researcher restored by re-deriving; and the printed units of the bias random-walk densities in (261)–(264) may differ in later revisions [unverified].

### Fossen/MSS matches Solà's convention but differs in five details

The book text was not read. The 2nd edition (Wiley 2021) splits the material into **Ch. 13 "Model-Based Navigation Systems" and Ch. 14 "Inertial Navigation Systems"**, according to the [FossenHandbook README](https://github.com/cybergalactic/FossenHandbook). Ch. 14.4 contains "Error-state Kalman Filter using Attitude Measurements" (14.4.2) and "Error-state Extended Kalman Filter with Attitude Estimation" (14.4.3), per a TOC snippet ([e-bookshelf sample](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-16324268-da82399760.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. The 2011 edition put everything in **Ch. 11 "Sensor and Navigation Systems"** [abstract/snippet]. MSS headers now cite a forthcoming 3rd edition (2027). For model-aided velocity, Ch. 13 is the relevant material; for the IMU-driven ESKF, Ch. 14.4 is [inference].

The quaternion ESKF in MSS, `ins_mekf.m` ([source](https://github.com/cybergalactic/MSS/blob/master/INS/functions/ins_mekf.m)) [MSS source], has a 15-D error state [δp δv δb_acc δa δb_ars]. The attitude error δa is twice the Gibbs vector, local. Biases are first-order Gauss–Markov with time constants T_acc and T_ars. Gravity is fixed WGS-84 g(μ) in NED. The update uses the Joseph form, multiplicative injection q ← q⊗δq̂, and **no reset Jacobian**. `Rquat`/`quatprod` use **scalar-first Hamilton** q = [η ε₁ ε₂ ε₃] mapping BODY→NED. So Solà's formulas carry over with G = NED and L = BODY. MSS's A-matrix blocks −R S(f) and −S(ω) equal Solà's (237b,c) [inference from the two sources]. Reusable marine aids in MSS are the magnetometer and gravity reference vectors, an average-sea-level integral pseudo-measurement, and a separate pressure-aided `ins_heave` ESKF. MSS has **no model-velocity aid**.

**Process-noise scaling flag (MSS).** In `ins_mekf`, E_d = h·E, so *all* process noise, including the bias-driving noise, scales as h²·Q_d. Solà scales sampled IMU noise by Δt² but bias random-walk noise by Δt (App. E, eqs. 439–443). **Tuning values cannot be moved between the two without checking how Q_d is defined in `SIMaidedINSquat`** [MSS source + inference]. The researchers also give a conversion from datasheet densities: V_i ≈ N_a²Δt, Θ_i ≈ N_g²Δt, A_i = σ_aw²Δt [inference, not printed by Solà].

Other notation hazards from the mapping note [inference]:
- **Symbol clashes:**
  - Solà's g is both gravity and the reset function.
  - Fossen's η is both the 6-DOF pose and the quaternion scalar.
  - Fossen's R_d is noise covariance, while Solà's R is rotation.
- **State ordering differs.** Permute before comparing F_x with A_d.
- **Body vs NED velocity.** Fossen's body velocity ν₁ = [u v w] must not be put into the INS state without the rotation v^n = R ν₁.

The researchers derived one result that is not in Solà [inference]. Take a body-frame water-relative model velocity h = Rᵀ(v^n − v_c^n) under the local error. Its measurement Jacobian has block Rᵀ on δv and [Rᵀ(v^n − v_c^n)]× on δθ. That has the same structure as MSS's magnetometer row. Checking it numerically against Solà's chain rule is a natural unit test.

### The plant model needed to link notation is not in the repository

The user referred to `auv_plant.m` as the Fossen-notation simulator to tie this notation to. **The AUV_Control repository currently contains no plant model.** It holds only `log/`, `reports/` and `research_notes/`, and no file matching "plant". ## The VN-200 must act as a raw sensor once the vehicle dives

### Groups 3, 5 and 6: use the uncompensated increments, and convert the quaternion

Field names and bit masks are confirmed from VectorNav's vnproglib `types.h` ([dawonn mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dawonn/vectornav/ros2/vectornav/vnproglib-1.2.0.0/cpp/include/vn/types.h)) [driver/SDK text]. Descriptions are quoted from the manual in INSTINCT's headers ([ImuOutputs.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UniStuttgart-INS/INSTINCT/main/src/util/Vendor/VectorNav/BinaryOutputs/ImuOutputs.hpp); [AttitudeOutputs.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UniStuttgart-INS/INSTINCT/main/src/util/Vendor/VectorNav/BinaryOutputs/AttitudeOutputs.hpp); [InsOutputs.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UniStuttgart-INS/INSTINCT/main/src/util/Vendor/VectorNav/BinaryOutputs/InsOutputs.hpp)) [driver/SDK text].

In **Group 3**, UncompAccel and UncompGyro carry static factory calibration only, "not compensated by any dynamic calibration such as bias compensation from the onboard INS Kalman filter". Accel and AngularRate do include the internal filter's bias estimates. DeltaTheta and DeltaVel are coning/sculling-integrated at 800 Hz. Their compensation is set by register DeltaThetaVelConfig (82): integration frame Body/NED, plus gyro, accel/gravity and earth-rate compensation options ([SDK register docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GSO-soslab/vectornav_python/main/vectornav_registers.md)) [driver/SDK text]. The DeltaTheta unit (degrees) came through a paraphrasing tool and should be checked. Pres is absolute pressure in kPa.

In **Group 5**, the Quaternion is **"The last term is the scalar value"**, body with respect to NED, i.e. [x, y, z, w]. **This conflicts with Solà and MSS, which are scalar-first, so an explicit reorder is required.** It is the component-order pitfall Solà warns is "prone to error". The DCM is column-major and maps NED→body, so it is the transpose of Fossen's R^n_b. YawPitchRoll is a 3-2-1 sequence. YprU is not valid in AHRS mode.

In **Group 6**, PosLla, VelNed and VelBody (whose reference point relative to InsRefOffset is unconfirmed) come with 1σ PosU and VelU.

The researchers' recommendation is to drive the external filter with the Uncomp or raw body-frame delta streams, not the internally bias-compensated ones. The compensated streams are correlated with the VN-200's own filter [inference].

### The internal INS stops updating position and velocity after about 45 s without GNSS

InsStatus mode 3 reads: "A GNSS outage has lasted more than 45 seconds. The INS Filter will no longer update the position and velocity outputs, but the attitude remains valid" ([INSTINCT VectorNavTypes.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UniStuttgart-INS/INSTINCT/main/src/util/Vendor/VectorNav/VectorNavTypes.hpp)) [driver/SDK text]. **Caveat:** the mode-0 wording mentions "GNSS Compass", so this text was taken from the **VN-300/VN-310 manual**. That it applies unchanged to current VN-200 firmware is **unconfirmed**. The ROS driver uses the same MODE_NO_GPS = 3 semantics ([InsStatus.msg](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dawonn/vectornav/ros2/vectornav_msgs/msg/InsStatus.msg)). If it holds, **the user's external filter has to do all submerged navigation.** The VN-200's position and velocity can be accepted only as surface measurements, gated on the mode bits [inference]. A product-brief snippet giving post-outage error growth of "3.0 cm/s²" is single-sourced and unverified ([brief](https://www.navtechgps.com/wp-content/uploads/VN200_ProductBrief_DS.pdf)).

Registers 50/51 (VelAidingMeas/VelAidingControl) exist in the SDK. However, their input frame, and whether the INS filter fuses them (as opposed to only compensating the AHRS accelerometer), are **unverified** ([SDK register docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GSO-soslab/vectornav_python/main/vectornav_registers.md)). They should not be relied on to inject the thrust-model velocity until UM2000 confirms what they do.

### The onboard barometer is not a depth sensor, so avoid fusing housing pressure

The VN-200 has an absolute barometer. Its range is quoted as **10–1200 mbar** ([datasheet snippet](https://metromatics.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/VN200CR-Datasheet-v3.3-DS200-CR-33-R1.pdf)) [abstract/snippet, single source]. That saturates at roughly 2 m of water. In a sealed housing it reads internal gas pressure, which changes with temperature and leaks, not with depth [inference]. Register 67 offers the scenarios **GnssInsWithPressure** and **GnssInsNoPressure** ([SDK register docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GSO-soslab/vectornav_python/main/vectornav_registers.md)) [driver/SDK text]. The internal INS should be configured as **GnssInsNoPressure** so that housing pressure is not fused as altitude [inference]. Pres remains useful as a leak and over-temperature monitor.

### Datasheet numbers set the planning scale for free-inertial drift

The following figures come from search snippets of the datasheets and briefs and must be checked against the user's exact variant (Rugged DS200-CR-33 v3.3 vs SMD DS200-SMD-30 v3.0) ([Rugged DS](https://metromatics.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/VN200CR-Datasheet-v3.3-DS200-CR-33-R1.pdf); [SMD DS](https://metromatics.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/VN200SMD-Datasheet-v3.0-DS200-SMD-30-R1.pdf); [brief](https://cornestech.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/VectorNav_VN-200_product_brief_CTL.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. Gyro: in-run bias <10 °/h (5 °/h typical), noise 0.0035 °/s/√Hz. Accelerometer: bias <0.04 mg, noise 0.14 mg/√Hz. Magnetometer noise: 140 µGauss/√Hz. Rates: IMU 800 Hz, navigation 400 Hz. Magnetic heading: 2.0° RMS. The current manual is **UM2000 r2**, which supersedes UM004 r2.22 ([Metromatics](https://metromatics.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/vn200-user-manual-um2000-r2.pdf)).

From these specs, accelerometer bias alone gives free-inertial error of ½·b·t². That is about 0.7 m after 60 s and about 70 m after 600 s, before any attitude-induced gravity leakage. Lab Allan figures should be inflated 2–10× for a vibrating AUV [inference]. This is the quantitative reason the thrust-model aid is essential.

## Without depth, the vertical channel and current are the unsolved problems

**Observability has not been analysed for this configuration.** The nearest analysis is Klein & Diamant, *Sensors* 2015, on DVL plus pressure-aided INS. A citing paper summarises it as showing that horizontal position is unobservable "for all maneuvers evaluated" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283303357_Observability_Analysis_of_DVLPS_Aided_INS_for_a_Maneuvering_AUV)) [metadata + snippet]. A model velocity is a velocity aid with worse, correlated error, so it gives at most the same observability. Without depth, the vertical channel is also unconstrained [inference].

The pure-inertial vertical channel is unstable, and in practice it is stabilised by a height or depth sensor ([Inside GNSS](https://insidegnss.com/inertial-error-propagation-understanding-inertial-behavior/)) [abstract/snippet]. **No AUV paper was found that navigates submerged without a depth sensor.** Candidate substitutes are all unvalidated [inference]: the model's heave velocity w; zero-vertical-velocity pseudo-measurements during depth hold; and depth ≈ 0 resets when GNSS is reacquired. MEMS ship-heave methods assume zero-mean oscillation, so they cannot track a sustained depth change.

**Current and model bias look the same on straight legs.** A constant NED current and a constant body-frame model-velocity bias separate only when heading changes [inference, standard argument]. One snippet states that "if the current is non-zero and unknown, the model is only weakly observable" (source paper not pinned) [abstract/snippet]. A GNSS fix on resurfacing constrains the time-integral of current plus model error. Separating those two terms needs several dives at different headings and speeds, and no paper proves this for MA-INS [inference]. Added mass matters only during acceleration, so it is likely poorly identifiable in-filter between surfacings [inference]. A flooded cavity makes this worse.

**Consistency is the other risk.** Huang, Mourikis & Roumeliotis show that EKF linearisation can make unobservable directions look observable, so covariance collapses spuriously. Their remedies are OC-EKF and FEJ ([IJRR 2010](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~stergios/papers/IJRR-Observability-based-rules-consistency-2010.pdf)) [abstract/snippet]. Model-aid error is strongly time-correlated. Feeding it as white noise at a high rate will make the filter overconfident [inference]. The textbook remedies for coloured noise are state augmentation and measurement differencing, which are equivalent for AR(1) noise ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254057826_Practical_Approaches_to_Kalman_Filtering_with_Time-Correlated_Measurement_Errors)), or a variance-inflation factor ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9521680/)) [abstract/snippet]. A model driven by measured gyro rates (Randeni-style) shares noise with the INS propagation; a model driven by thrust commands avoids that [inference]. The researchers suggest a test without ground truth: the normalised innovation of the **first GNSS fix after each dive**, checked against χ²(2) across many dives [inference]. Gating fixes biases that statistic low ([arXiv 2512.18508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18508)) [abstract/snippet].

The T200 curves are published at fixed supply voltages. Battery sag therefore shows up to the filter as a slowly varying thrust-gain bias, unless voltage is an input to the thrust model or a thrust-gain state is carried [inference]. The PWM interface runs 1100–1900 µs with a deadband around 1500 µs ([Blue Robotics](https://bluerobotics.com/store/thrusters/t100-t200-thrusters/t200-thruster-r2-rp/)) [abstract/snippet].

### Consolidated open gaps

No published work was found on any of the following. Each could be a contribution:
- Observability of INS + model velocity + magnetometer + intermittent surface GNSS **without depth**.
- Submerged navigation with no pressure sensor.
- Identifiability of hydrodynamic and added-mass parameters inside a navigation filter with no DVL or ADCP. Flooded-cavity added mass is unaddressed.
- NEES/NIS consistency evaluation of any model-aided INS, including resurfacing-innovation consistency.
- The correlation time of model-aid velocity error.
- Model-aided drift numbers for a hovering 6-DOF or BlueROV-class vehicle.
- Use of a T200 thrust model as a navigation aid.

Separately, the 3-D sensor suites of Ji 2023, Allotta 2016 and Randeni 2018 remain unverified, so it is still possible that one of them had no depth sensor.

## Conclusion

The literature establishes the mechanism, but not the user's regime. A thrust/hydrodynamic velocity aid in an error-state quaternion filter is a known, effective technique. Its documented weak points are parameter error, unknown current and coloured model error, and each has a published mitigation. Every one of those mitigations, however, was demonstrated with a depth sensor, and usually with an acoustic or Doppler reference as well. The user's real research question is therefore less "does model-aiding work" than "what can be estimated at all when the only external information is a GNSS fix at each surfacing". That question has not been analysed for this configuration: no observability analysis, no identifiability result for current versus thrust-model bias, and no consistency method. It is an opening for a contribution, and also the main risk.

Two choices set the shape of everything else: whether to stay depth-free, and how many model and current states to carry. Before either choice, the full texts of JOE 2011, Ji 2023 and the VN-200 UM2000 manual should be read to confirm the assumptions this review could only take from abstracts.
